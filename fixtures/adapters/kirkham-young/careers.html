This is a newly created senior role within a leading healthcare organisation, focused on delivering meaningful change across complex NHS care pathways.

Sitting within the commercial organisation, but working closely with Market Access, Government Affairs, Health Economics, sales and clinical teams, you will help identify and remove the regional barriers that prevent patients accessing appropriate care closer to home.

This is not a traditional sales position. It would suit someone from Market Access, NHS strategic partnerships, healthcare transformation, commissioning, Government Affairs or pathway development who has successfully turned strategy into measurable change.

The role

You will work across England, initially with a particular focus on London and the Midlands, engaging with commissioners, NHS stakeholders and clinical teams to:

  • Identify barriers and bottlenecks within existing patient pathways
  • Influence regional commissioning and funding decisions
  • Develop practical solutions that support pathway redesign
  • Build compelling clinical, financial and patient-focused cases for change
  • Navigate complex NHS stakeholder groups and decision-making structures
  • Work collaboratively with commercial, clinical, Market Access, Health Economics and Government Affairs colleagues
  • Develop long-term strategic partnerships capable of delivering sustainable healthcare transformation

This is a long-term strategic role. Success will not simply be measured by immediate increases in patient numbers, but by achieving key milestones, unlocking funding, changing pathways and creating the foundations for significant future growth.

What we’re looking for

We are interested in people who understand how change actually happens within the NHS.

You may currently work within Market Access, strategic healthcare partnerships, commissioning, NHS transformation, Government Affairs or a similar healthcare strategy role.

Most importantly, you will be able to demonstrate:

  • Experience influencing NHS stakeholders at regional or system level
  • A strong understanding of commissioning, funding and healthcare pathways
  • Evidence of delivering genuine change rather than purely developing strategy
  • The ability to build relationships within complex healthcare ecosystems
  • Strong commercial awareness combined with an understanding of clinical, patient and financial outcomes
  • The resilience and credibility to engage stakeholders where access and change can take time

Experience within homecare or renal services would be useful but is not essential. A broader understanding of complex NHS pathways and how to influence change is more important.

This is a rare opportunity to join at the beginning of a significant long-term healthcare transformation programme, with further roles expected to follow as the strategy develops.
